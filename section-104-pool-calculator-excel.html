Section 104 pool calculator for crypto, in Excel

Bought the same coin at five different prices? HMRC doesn't use any one of them. It uses your average cost across every purchase, which is the "Section 104 pool". Here's how it works, how to build it in Excel, and where a simple version goes wrong.

What the Section 104 pool is

For each type of token you hold, HMRC treats all your coins as one pot with one total cost (CRYPTO22200). Every purchase adds coins and cost to the pot. Every sale takes out coins, along with a proportional slice of the cost.

Allowable costs like exchange fees are added to the cost when you buy, and deducted from proceeds when you sell.

The Excel formulas

For a single token, with trades in date order (column B = "Buy" or "Sell", C = quantity, D = £ amount including fees), the running pool is:

ColumnMeaningFormula (row 3)
EPool quantity=E2 + IF(B3="Buy", C3, -C3)
FCost taken out on a sale=IF(B3="Sell", G2 * C3 / E2, 0)
GPool cost=G2 + IF(B3="Buy", D3, -F3)
HGain on a sale=IF(B3="Sell", D3 - F3, "")

Row 2 holds your opening pool (zero if you're starting fresh). Copy row 3 down for every trade.

Why this simple sheet isn't enough on its own: the pool is the last of three matching steps. Before any sale touches the pool, HMRC matches it with coins bought the same day, then with coins bought in the next 30 days. Those matched coins never enter the pool. A pure average-cost sheet gets the answer wrong whenever you've bought back within 30 days. See the 30-day rule worked example.

Other things the pool has to handle

  • Swaps: swapping ETH for SOL is a sale of ETH (out of the ETH pool) and a purchase of SOL (into the SOL pool), both valued in pounds at the time.
  • Gas fees paid in crypto: spending ETH on gas is itself a small disposal of ETH.
  • Foreign currency: trades in USD or EUR need converting to pounds on the day. Gainsheet uses Bank of England daily rates.
  • One pool per token, not one pool per exchange. BTC on Coinbase and BTC on Kraken go in the same pool.
  • Transfers between your own wallets aren't disposals, so they don't touch the pool (though network fees may).
