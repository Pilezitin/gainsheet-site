A UK crypto tax spreadsheet for 2025/26

Plenty of people try to work out their crypto tax in a spreadsheet, and plenty of those spreadsheets give the wrong answer. Not because the maths is hard, but because HMRC's rules aren't the ones most people assume. Here's what a spreadsheet has to do to get it right, and a ready-made one if you'd rather not build it.

Do you even need to report?

For the 2025/26 tax year (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026), you generally need to report crypto on your Self Assessment return if:

  • your total gains are more than the £3,000 annual exempt amount, or
  • your total disposal proceeds are more than £50,000, even if you made no gain, or
  • you want to claim a loss (losses must be claimed within 4 years to be usable).

Capital gains tax on crypto is 18% within the basic-rate band and 24% above it. The deadline for the online return is 31 January 2027.

What counts as a disposal

Not just selling for pounds. Each of these is a disposal that needs a pound value (CRYPTO22100):

  • selling crypto for pounds or any other currency;
  • swapping one token for another, including into stablecoins;
  • spending crypto on goods or services, including gas fees;
  • giving crypto away (other than to a spouse or civil partner).

Moving coins between your own wallets or exchanges isn't a disposal.

Building your own vs using a ready-made one

If you have a handful of trades on one exchange, a careful DIY sheet is fine: the Section 104 formulas are only a few columns. It gets painful when you have:

  • hundreds of trades, or several exchanges;
  • swaps between tokens (each swap is a sale and a buy);
  • buy-backs within 30 days of selling;
  • trades in currencies other than pounds.

FAQ

Does it work in Google Sheets?

No. It's built for desktop Microsoft Excel (2010 or later) and uses no macros.

Does it handle DeFi?

There's a DeFi tab for entering on-chain transactions by hand, with a short guide. Whether a particular DeFi action is a disposal is your judgement; HMRC's position is in CRYPTO61000 onwards. If you have thousands of on-chain transactions, wallet-syncing software is a better fit.

Is this tax advice?

No. It's a calculation tool that applies HMRC's published rules to the data you give it. For your own position, check HMRC's guidance or ask an accountant.
